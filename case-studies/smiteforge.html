UX / UI DESIGN · MOBILE APP · CONCEPT

SmiteForge

A Smite 2 companion app designed for iOS and Android without making either feel like the other.

Mobile companion app · Sole designer

WHY
01 / why this exists

Smite information existed. Getting to it quickly on a phone was the problem.

Fan resources existed, but they were cluttered and built for desktop.

SmiteForge explored what quick, mobile-first reference could look like instead, checked mid-match or between games, not dug through on a laptop later.

3 player interviews→ 1 preference survey→ 2 platform languages
SIGNAL
02 / the signal

I spoke with three Smite players, ran a feature-preference survey, and worked with one recurring tester throughout the project.

the insight

Different depth, same ask: get out of the way.

DECISIONS
03 / key decisions
01

One brand, two platform languages

The content and identity stayed consistent. The interface language changed with the platform.

On iOS: SF Pro, softer containers, generous edge spacing. A lighter, more fluid interface.

On Android: Roboto, stronger blocks, depth and color. A more structured hierarchy.

These are design interpretations informed by each platform, not claims about universal iOS or Android rules.

Both builds are shown in full further down, side by side.

02

Make it feel connected to Smite

Gold and blue echo Smite’s own branding, so the app feels connected to the game instead of like a generic stats tool.

The interface system still gives SmiteForge its own structure.

This project references visual content from the Smite universe, sourced from public fan and wiki material. I don’t own that content. Original icons are my own design, labeled as such.

03

Testing changed the interface

Testing surfaced two real problems. Each led to a specific change.

observationdesign response
Users couldn’t immediately tell what SmiteForge was for
→
added a short onboarding sequence introducing the product before dropping players into the interface
Logged-out users tried to save builds and weren’t sure why it failed
→
disabled the Save action until login, with a clear visual treatment

Both fixes are visible in the platform boards above: the onboarding screen in the top row, and the Save Build sheet with its Log In and Sign Up options at the bottom.

PLATFORMS
04 / side by side

The same core tasks, carried across both versions. Typography, spacing, containers, and hierarchy adapted to each platform.

STATUS
05 / where it stands

Concept work, not shipped.

SmiteForge stayed a concept project, but it changed how I think about cross-platform design. Consistency doesn’t require identical interfaces. The product can stay the same while each platform speaks its own language.

UX / UI DESIGN · MOBILE APP · CONCEPT

Zentra

Save with clarity. Spend with purpose.

Mobile product · UX/UI · Sole designer, end to end

WHY
01 / why this exists

Saving money is easy to measure and surprisingly hard to feel.

Financial tools can tell you what you spent or what's left.

Zentra explored something more motivating: connecting those numbers to the things someone is actually saving for, then making progress visible enough to keep moving.

2 personas→ 1 shared need
SIGNAL
02 / the signal

Two people, two very different relationships with money, same underlying need.

flexibilitySamantha · the freelancer

The freelancer needs a system that bends.

Irregular income makes rigid financial planning harder to maintain.

“I want saving to feel rewarding, not like punishment.”

clarity, without judgmentJason · the grad student

The grad student needs an honest, simple overview.

Limited money makes every decision matter, but the interface shouldn't make financial stress worse.

“I want to stay on track, not get lectured about my spending.”

the insight

Different financial situations, same ask: make the progress visible, and never make it feel like a lecture.

DECISIONS
03 / key decisions
01

Progress as the product

Zentra organizes saving around goals, not one abstract balance. A vacation, an emergency fund, a wedding, a car.

Each becomes something visible and measurable instead of disappearing into one total.

Progress rings, percentages, and dollar amounts turn "I should save more" into something concrete: where I am now and how far I have left.

02

Identity built around momentum

Zentra needed to feel like progress, not paperwork.

The cheetah mark runs. Deep green and gold carry growth and achievement, and the mark appears in motion wherever the app is working.

Nunito Sans for headlines and Open Sans for body keep the type rounded and readable, so the product can feel credible without intimidating someone who has never budgeted a day in their life.

03

Testing changed the interface

Three usability sessions surfaced real friction. Each one led to a specific change.

observationdesign response

Accessibility and contrast were part of the design review, not treated as separate from it.

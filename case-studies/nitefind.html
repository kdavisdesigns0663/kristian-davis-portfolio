Nitefind

Role · Sole UX/UI designer Duration · 10 weeks Status · Concept, high-fidelity prototype

Why this exists

Choosing where to go out shouldn't take longer than actually going out. Nitefind exists to cut the decision fatigue people feel when they're trying to pick a venue — too many options, unreliable information, no fast way to know if a place actually matches the night they want to have.

The signal

User interviews surfaced "decision fatigue" as the real, named problem — not a lack of venues, but too many low-confidence options and no fast way to filter them by vibe. That reframed the brief from "build a directory" to "build a fast decision tool."

Key decisions

Two directions were explored early: Midnight Grid — sleek, minimal, dark, built for clarity — and Alt Scene — bold, eclectic, rebellious, built to celebrate nightlife subculture. Alt Scene was the more visually exciting option. Midnight Grid was chosen instead, because the entire product thesis was reducing decision fatigue through clarity — and a louder, busier direction would have worked against the problem the app was actually solving, however good it looked on its own.

A related, smaller test: a faint grid texture was tried behind the navigation and tested against plain black with a small pool of users. Plain black won clearly — the hypothesis going in was that the texture would add visual noise without benefit, and the result confirmed it. Small test, single variable, straightforward result — but the same "verify, don't assume" discipline that shaped the bigger typography and palette decisions.

Where it stands

Concept work with a complete, tested design system — not shipped to real users at scale. Honest next step: validate the typography choice (Orbitron reads closer to gaming/sci-fi than the calm, decision-reducing tone the rest of the product argues for) and resolve the peer-review notes on density and navigation before treating this as production-ready.
